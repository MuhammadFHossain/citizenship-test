Study guide

The interview and how to prepare

Ten to fifteen minutes a day is enough. Do the daily questions, take a practice test a few times a week, and check the answers that change before your interview.

The civics test is spoken. The officer reads each question aloud and you answer aloud. There are no answer choices. This app uses multiple choice only while you first learn a question.

The four parts of the interview

  1. SpeakingConversation with the officerThe officer reviews your N-400 application with you. Your answers serve as the speaking test.
  2. ReadingRead one sentence correctlyYou read a sentence aloud. You have up to three tries.
  3. WritingWrite one sentence correctlyThe officer reads a sentence and you write it down. You have up to three tries.
  4. Civics12 correct out of up to 20The test ends when you reach 12 correct or 9 incorrect. Under the 65/20 rule: 10 questions from the starred list, 6 correct to pass.

If you fail a part, you get one retest, usually 60 to 90 days later, on that part only.

Which test you take

It depends on the date you filed Form N-400.

  • On or after October 20, 2025: the 2025 test, with 128 questions. This app covers that test.
  • Before October 20, 2025: the 2008 test, with 100 questions. The officer asks 10 and you need 6 correct.

How to study

  • Answer before you look. Recalling an answer builds memory faster than rereading it.
  • Review on a schedule. A question you answer correctly comes back after 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 days. A question you miss comes back the same day.
  • Speak your answers. The test is oral, so practice saying each answer aloud.
  • Take practice tests. Once you have seen every question, take one every two or three days, and every day in your last week.

Missing a day is fine. Reviews wait for you.

Answers that change

These depend on elections and where you live. Confirm them on the USCIS test updates page the week of your interview.
